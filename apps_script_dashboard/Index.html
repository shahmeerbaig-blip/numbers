Pattern 1

Location change → delay → refund

Orders where the dropoff pin shifted materially between dispatch and actual delivery, the shift coincided with a delivery delay, and the customer then filed a late-delivery / wrong-address refund or compensation claim on that order.

How this is calculated

Flags a primary delivery where the distance between the dispatch-time expected dropoff and the actual dropoff exceeds the pin-shift threshold, AND the order's recorded delivery delay exceeds the delay threshold, AND the same order has a compensation/refund event with a late-delivery or wrong-address contact reason. Rows are joined on the order's global order ID.

Pattern 2

Repeat-offender risk score

Customers ranked 0–100 on trailing refund/compensation claim frequency + EUR value, across all claim reasons, tiered LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH.

How this is calculated

For each customer with at least the minimum claim count, ranks them by percentile on claim count and percentile on total EUR claimed, blends the two 50/50 into a 0–100 score, and tiers HIGH (≥90), MEDIUM (≥70), LOW (below). Deliberately a transparent, reviewable blend rather than a black-box model. The score counts every claim reason equally, including partner/vendor-initiated cancellations — which aren't the customer's fault the way a self-reported missing/wrong-item claim is. The "Item claims" and "Cancellation claims" columns split each customer's count/value so you can see which is actually driving a high score before acting on it.

Pattern 3 · new

Fraud network — active accounts linked to known fraud

Active (not yet blocked) accounts that Shield has already linked, by shared device or mobile number, to at least one confirmed-fraud account — the highest-confidence "same person, new account" candidates. Not date-ranged: this reads Shield's current daily snapshot.

How this is calculated, and why not by delivery location

Sourced directly from agg_customer_braze_fraud_flag (network_shield_fraud_account_count), which Shield computes from shared device IDs and mobile numbers — filtered to accounts not already blocked. We tested matching by shared dropoff GPS coordinates instead (same idea: same person re-registering after a block) and it was too noisy to ship: at a 15m location grid, Qatar had 46,935 locations used by both a blocked and an active customer over 180 days — almost all shared apartment buildings, not fraud. Device/mobile linkage doesn't have that false-positive problem.
